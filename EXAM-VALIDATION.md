# CCNA Launchpad v1.5.0 — exam validation work plan

Status: **unvalidated draft**. A 100-item, 120-minute rehearsal is implemented. Its size and single-answer format are a product choice, not a claim about the exact number/type of questions on Cisco's exam. Items are original, not exam dumps. The published v2.0 blueprint supplies the planning domains, not an endorsement.

## v1.5.0 revision

The new R form contains revised versions of the original 100 scenarios with immutable v150 IDs. Original items remain for saved F/A/B attempts. Earlier item exposure is mapped to the revised form; revised wording is not independent assessment. New practice uses separate lesson scenarios. The revised options remove absurd distractors, but remain author-reviewed drafts. Missed/unanswered results appear first with an optional filter. The current session and ten previous completed sessions are retained.

## Earlier baseline evidence

- The full form has 25/25/20/20/10 questions across the five domains and samples all 29 top-level objectives. This does not establish complete subobjective coverage.
- Pilot A/B retain their original stable IDs. The full form contains those 40 items and 60 additional draft items. Previous form exposure is retained and displayed.
- Automated checks verify distinct IDs, valid answer keys, nonduplicate options, objective mapping, domain proportions, option permutation, scoring, deadline enforcement, persistence and export.
- Original explanations and misconception categories accompany every item. The added items include configuration and operational evidence interpretation. They are not performance-based configuration tasks.
- Attempt exports contain the item ID, objective, selected/expected answer, exposure metadata and timing. Exports are opt-in downloads, not automatic telemetry.

## Required before changing the validation claim

1. **Independent technical review.** A networking practitioner other than the author must review every item against authoritative documentation and, where needed, reproduce the scenario in a named simulator/device image. Record reviewer, date, environment, correct answer, ambiguity findings and disposition. Do not infer approval from a completed software test.
2. **Blueprint review.** Map each subobjective, required action verb and intended depth to instruction, practical work and assessment. Explain every uncovered subobjective. Top-level ID coverage is insufficient.
3. **Novice usability review.** Observe intended learners using the exam controls without coaching. Review terminology, screen-reader/keyboard operation, mobile readability, unnecessary language difficulty and accommodations. Provide an untimed practice alternative before claiming equitable timed interpretation.
4. **Representative learner pilot.** Recruit learners with varied relevant preparation, obtain consent for any data collection, and record prior exposure. Use a separate held-out form for later transfer. Reusing the same 100-item form cannot establish an independent readiness score.
5. **Item analysis.** Examine proportion correct, distractor selection, omissions, completion times and relationships between items and overall performance. Small or selected samples make these estimates uncertain. Negative discrimination or unused distractors trigger review, not automatic deletion. Use qualified assessment review to set the sampling/analysis plan; no arbitrary sample size guarantees validity.
6. **Revision and re-pilot.** Revise ambiguous or ineffective items, give changed meaning a new ID, retain the review history, and collect fresh evidence. Any pass threshold requires a defensible standard-setting process rather than an invented percentage.
7. **Independent outcome comparison.** Evaluate transfer with unseen practicals and independent reputable assessments. Do not market certification pass prediction without evidence supporting that specific claim.

## Review record template

For each item record: item ID/revision; objective/subobjective; intended skill; authoritative source; reproduced configuration/output if relevant; reviewer/date; ambiguity and alternative-answer checks; distractor rationale; accessibility concerns; disposition; required revision; second-review outcome.

For each practical record: device/simulator version; topology and starter configuration; commands actually executed; expected and observed outputs; positive and negative traffic tests; injected fault; recovery verification; reviewer; remaining limitations.

## What is deliberately not claimed

No expert endorsement, calibrated difficulty, reliable pass threshold, equivalent forms, full performance-based coverage, completed learner pilot, or secure examination environment. Local browser data and answer keys can be inspected or altered. This is a personal learning tool.
